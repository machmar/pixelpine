# PixelPine product firmware

Runs Pine on the PY32F003F18P6: the 15 light shows, his face and 18 behaviours, the 6 transitions, the touch star,
his brain and his bond, as the simulator ([`simulator/index.html`](../../simulator/index.html)) does. It's a port of
the simulator's JavaScript, read side by side: the C names follow the JS ones. Written and brought up on the first
rev A board on 2026-11-03.

**Status:** running on the board. The PC build of the same C code is checked frame by frame against the simulator's
own JavaScript (see [Matching the simulator](#matching-the-simulator)). Touch is calibrated on one board and one
person. Room-light sensing turned out to be impossible on rev A (see [Room light](#room-light)), and a whole-tree
touch field was tried and dropped (see [Touch field](#touch-field-tried-dropped)).

## Build and flash

Needs the Arm GNU Toolchain (14.2), Python 3 and, for the tests, Node.js and MSYS2's mingw64 gcc. Flashing needs
the J-Link setup in [`../flash/`](../flash/README.md).

```bash
python gen_tables.py
```

```bash
python build.py flash
```

| Command | Builds |
|---|---|
| `python build.py` | product firmware, `build/pixelpine.hex` |
| `python build.py flash` | the same, then programs the board through the J-Link |
| `python build.py host` | the PC test build, `build/pine_host.exe` |
| `python build.py showcycle` | bring-up: every show for 8 s in turn, no brain |
| `python build.py bench` | CPU-time bench; keeps SWD alive (lines L8/L9 dark), read with `test/readbench.py` |
| `python build.py diag` | LED test patterns, 6 s each: all off / top LED full / top LED dim / all at 64 |
| `python build.py calib` | product firmware with SWD alive, for `test/sensemon.py`, `calibrate.py`, `gestures.py`, `stackcheck.py` |
| `python build.py touchrec` | product firmware, LEDs lit, recording the first 10 touch episodes; after 180 s the LEDs go dark and SWD comes back, read with `test/touchrec.py` |

To flash without the firmware ever running with the probe on its LED lines:
`python ../flash/flash.py build/pixelpine.hex --hold 20` keeps NRST low for 20 s after programming; unplug SWDIO
and SWCLK meanwhile.

The product firmware turns SWDIO/SWCLK into LED lines L8/L9, so the debugger can only reach it through
connect-under-reset (which `flash.py` does).

| Resource | Used | Of |
|---|---|---|
| Flash | 59 084 B (92 %) | 63 KB (the last 1 KB holds Pine's bond) |
| RAM, static | 7 036 B | 8 KB |
| Stack, deepest measured | 464 B (stack painting, `test/stackcheck.py`, 8-bit scan) | 1 156 B left for it |

## How it maps onto the hardware

- **Scan:** 16 anode phases x 10-bit BCM, 1 us base slot: 16 x 512 x 1 us = 8.2 ms, **122 Hz** refresh. Each LED
  gets round(1023 x brightness^2.2). Level bits 1..9 get slots of 1 .. 256 us; level bit 0 gets a 1 us slot of its
  own on every other refresh only (61 Hz), so it weighs 0.5 us on average and costs no refresh rate. Bits 0-3 are
  shown from the phase-start interrupt (an assembler burst with counted delays, then the timer for bit 3); TIM1
  interrupts once per bit 4..9. Masks are rebuilt every frame, one anode at a time with the interrupt held off.
  The simulator's "Raw scan, 8 bit" model and the comparisons below stay at 8 bits.
- **Why 10 bits (2026-11-04):** with 8 bits the dimmest step is 1/255 of full duty, and on the board pixels visibly
  popped on and off at the dim end of every fade (the morning face at boot, every change of face). At 10 bits the
  first step is 1/1023: a pixel lights from 3 % brightness instead of 6 %, with 4x finer steps above it. The
  counted delays assume single-cycle GPIO stores (the IOPORT): measured on the board, 64 stores = 70 cycles.
- **Scan pitfalls found on the board (2026-11-03):**
  - **Pull resistors:** after reset PA13 (SWDIO) has a pull-up and PA14 (SWCLK) a pull-down. As lines L8/L9 they
    kept ~30 LEDs glowing faintly even with every LED at 0. `scan_init()` turns the pulls off on all 16 lines.
  - **Timer wrap:** if a bit-slot interrupt runs late, the new period can be shorter than the count already
    reached and the 16-bit timer runs to 65 535 (2.7 ms): bright flashes, worst in the radar. Guarded in the ISR.
  - **Latched phases:** each anode phase copies its masks at its start, so a frame update mid-phase can't mix
    old and new bits.
  - **Empty slots:** a slot with no LED lit doesn't drive the anode (it would charge every floating line
    through the anode's LEDs, a faint flash on each).
  - **Current-sharing compensation** (the simulator's "Compensated" model) is in `scan.c` behind `-DSHARE_COMP`
    but off: on the board, and in the simulator's own model, it made levels jitter frame to frame.
  - **Debug builds never light LEDs:** in the `calib` / `bench` / `listen` builds SWDIO/SWCLK (lines L8/L9) stay
    on the probe, which holds SWDIO high, so every LED from L8 to a line the scan pulls low lit at full current
    (two "stuck LED" incidents). Those builds now keep every mask empty. All builds run the watchdog, and the
    crash handler floats every line first.
  - **Never halt a running board:** a halted core freezes the scan with one slot's pins driven, so one LED
    gets steady current (~8-9 mA, bright but within the LED's rating). Measure with connect-under-reset or the
    `calib` / `bench` builds without halting, and use `python build.py diag` for test patterns.
  - **Left over:** at 5 V a tiny current passes through two LEDs in series, so a lit LED can make one other
    LED glow very softly. Rev B fix: run the board a little below 5 V (a series diode, ~4.3 V).
- **Clock:** internal 24 MHz HSI (no PLL on the F003), 0 flash wait states. SysTick ticks the 50 fps frame.
- **Watchdog:** independent watchdog on the LSI (which has to be switched on first on the PY32), ~1 s, refreshed
  every frame; product build only, so the debug builds can halt. Checked with a test build (`-DKEEP_SWD
  -DWDT_TEST`): a deliberate hang from the debugger rebooted Pine after ~1 s.
- **Version:** `"PixelPine PP-240-WW fw 0.1.0"` at 0x080000C0, right after the vector table, readable over SWD.
- **Frame budget:** a frame is 20 ms. Most frames take 4-12 ms; a 205 s run of the normal firmware (calib build)
  had no late frame and a worst frame of 19.0 ms. A transition between the two heaviest shows with a
  face overlay can take up to ~24 ms; the main loop then runs the next frame's logic without rebuilding the LED
  levels, so animation timing stays locked to the simulator's frame count and the LEDs hold their levels for 20 ms.
  Measured on the board (bench build, 24 MHz):

| Show | one frame alone | | Show | one frame alone |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|
| Twinkle | 2.3 ms | | Fill & drain | 4.1 ms |
| Snowfall | 1.6 ms (+ flakes, ~5 ms full) | | Northern lights | 8.1 ms |
| Breathe | 1.6 ms | | Fireworks | 3.8 ms |
| Spiral garland | 4.3 ms | | Ornaments | 3.7 ms |
| Candle flicker | 8.5 ms | | Soft radar | 2.9 ms |
| Star ripple | 4.7 ms | | Heartbeat | 1.9 ms |
| Fireflies | 1.3 ms | | Tinsel shimmer | 3.3 ms |
| Rising sparks | 2.6 ms | | Face overlay / face scene | ~1-4 ms |

- **Numbers:** per-LED loops run in fixed point (Q12, table sin / exp / pow). The brain, the face behaviours and
  the per-frame scalars stay in `double`, in the JS's order of operations, so their decisions are the same. Soft
  double on this core costs ~310 cycles per add or multiply, so particle positions (snow, sparks, fireworks,
  fireflies) moved to fixed point too.

## Matching the simulator

`python test/compare.py` builds the PC version and runs it next to `test/simref.js`, which loads
`simulator/index.html` into Node with the page stubbed out. Both get the same seeds, the same random-number stream
(`Math.random` replaced by the firmware's xorshift32) and the same touch input (the simulator's recorded gesture
signals). What is compared is what the LEDs get: the 8-bit level after gamma 2.2. Results on 2026-11-03:

`Math.sin`, `Math.cos` and `%`: **bit-identical** to V8 on 354 000 arguments.

**Whole Pine from power-on** (brain + faces + scenes + transitions + touch):

| Scenario | Length | State machine | Emotions | LED levels within 1 step |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|
| Left alone, 1x clock | 120 s | identical every frame | identical (diff 0) | 100.00 % |
| Left alone, 10x clock | 180 s | identical every frame (morning > excited > work > daydream > reminded > work > daydream > doze > sleep > morning > work) | identical | 99.99 % |
| Touched a lot (hover, tap, pet, press, squeeze, tickle) | 90 s | identical every frame | identical | 99.99 % |
| Dark room, woken up, 30x clock | 120 s | identical every frame | identical | 100.00 % |

**Each light show alone, 30 s:**

| Show | exact | within 1 step | | Show | exact | within 1 step |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---:|---:|
| Twinkle | 97.0 % | 100.00 % | | Fill & drain | 96.8 % | 99.37 % |
| Snowfall | 99.0 % | 99.99 % | | Northern lights | 80.7 % | 99.43 % |
| Breathe | 94.5 % | 100.00 % | | Fireworks | 99.8 % | 100.00 % |
| Spiral garland | 95.3 % | 99.93 % | | Ornaments | 99.8 % | 100.00 % |
| Candle flicker | 85.6 % | 100.00 % | | Soft radar | 96.7 % | 100.00 % |
| Star ripple | 98.5 % | 100.00 % | | Heartbeat | 93.8 % | 100.00 % |
| Fireflies | 89.0 % | 93.31 % | | Tinsel shimmer | 93.0 % | 98.62 % |
| Rising sparks | 99.6 % | 99.93 % | | | | |

Where it differs, and why:

- **Fireflies** follow the same steering rules, but their paths are chaotic: after ~5 s the fixed-point flies are
  somewhere else than the simulator's (the first 5 s match 97.5 %). The double-precision version matched to the
  end but cost 17 ms a frame.
- **Snowfall**: about once in 30 s a flake lands one frame earlier or on the neighbouring column (fixed-point flake
  positions), so that LED of the snow pile differs until it melts.
- **Northern lights, candle, tinsel, fill**: Q12 rounding in long chains of noise and powers; never more than 5
  steps of 255.
- The simulator opens mid-yawn (it pre-runs 175 frames for the preview); the tree starts the yawn from the beginning.
- Pine's clock is the simulator's "1x like the real tree".

## Touch calibration

Charge sharing between the star (PA7 through R19, 1 kOhm) and the ADC's sample capacitor. A touching finger also
brings mains hum: the board's USB supply floats relative to you, and sampled at exactly 50 Hz the hum turns into a
slow beat of several volts. So one sample is taken at every anode change of the scan (~39 per 20 ms frame) and the
frame uses their mean. Two PY32 details: the ADC clears ADEN after every conversion (re-enabled per conversion), and
ADEN is ignored for a few clocks after calibration.

Measured on the first board (`test/calibrate.py`, 2026-11-03, mean reading per frame, x8):

| Gesture | Reading | Share of untouched |
|---|---:|---:|
| Untouched | 8 510 +- 15 | 1.00 |
| Finger resting on the front | ~3 500-3 900 | ~0.44 |
| Firm press, flattened fingertip | ~2 200-2 500 | ~0.27 |
| Pinch through front and back | ~1 650-1 800 | ~0.20 |
| Finger 1 cm above | no change | 1.00 |

`hw/sense.c` maps the ratio piecewise-linearly onto the simulator's scale, with its thresholds halfway between
these (touch 0.35 at a 18 % drop; press and squeeze since recalibrated, below). The touch threshold is low on purpose:
contact takes a few hundred ms to build up, and a quick tap still has to count.
A gesture test (`test/gestures.py`) recognised tap, tickle, pet and squeeze. A baseline tracker follows drift
(~10 s), and something resting on the star for 30 s becomes the new baseline.

**Squeeze vs press (changed 2026-11-04, in the simulator too).** On the board a squeeze often looked like a tap.
The touch recorder (`build.py touchrec`) showed why. Eight natural squeezes, held 2-3.5 s, all crossed the press
level 80-260 ms in, and then took 0.4-1.3 s more to reach squeeze level. So every one fired "press" first, and
Pine answers a press like a tap while working or daydreaming. Two of the eight never reached squeeze level at all
(peaks 0.74 and 0.95). The classifier now decides a press on release, or after 1.5 s held in the press band. A
touch that reached the press band is never a pet. Replayed on the recording: 6 of 8 squeezes are clean squeezes
(none were before), one is a long press and one a pet. The squeeze threshold stays where it is: lowering it for
the two weak squeezes would need press recordings to check that firm presses don't become squeezes.

**Recalibrated for a free-standing board (2026-11-04).** With that fix flashed, squeeze stopped working altogether.
The table above, and the first recording, were measured with the J-Link attached, and its ground ties the board
to the PC, which makes the mains hum, and so the drop, bigger. A second recording with every probe wire unplugged:
five squeezes peaked at r = 0.27-0.34 (not ~0.20), all short of the old squeeze level (0.235). `hw/sense.c` now
puts press at r = 0.47 and squeeze at r = 0.38. On a laptop's USB port (grounded through the PC) touches read
deeper, so there a firm press can count as a squeeze: the product is calibrated for a wall charger.

**Hover can't be sensed** with this electrode: a finger 1 cm away changes the reading by less than the noise. The
gesture stays in the firmware (it fires if a finger comes very close).

## Room light

The simulator's "room lights off, Pine gets sleepy" needs the tree to see the room light. The plan was to use the
LEDs as photodiodes, but that **can't work in a full charlieplex matrix**: every pair of lines has two LEDs, one
each way round. Reverse-biasing one LED forward-biases its partner, which clamps the line (that is what the first
attempt measured: the same ~2.1 V with lights on and off), and without bias the two LEDs' photocurrents cancel.
So rev A keeps the simulator's default, lights on: Pine still gets tired, dozes and sleeps on his own energy, but
there is no "sleepy in the dark" and no brightness adaptation. Left alone at the real 1x clock (PC build, three
seeds, 75 min): he dozes off ~15 min after power-on, sleeps ~2.5 min, then the cycle repeats every ~27 min. The
simulator's default clock is 10x, so there it looks ten times faster.

**Fix for rev B, at no cost:** leave one LED unpopulated (for example the L1 -> L0 one), so its partner L0 -> L1
has no anti-parallel twin and works as a photodiode between frames. The tree loses one pixel.

## Touch field (tried, dropped)

The idea: use the 240-LED matrix itself as a touch surface, so a fingertip anywhere on the tree counts (Pine would
look toward it, and a tap during a light show would send a ripple out from the finger). Tried on the board
2026-11-04 in five experiments and three runs of a full driver, then dropped: **the signal depends on how the tree
is powered**, and on the supplies a customer would actually use it is too weak to be reliable.

| Run | Method | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lines as capacitive electrodes (charge sharing, RC time) | A fingertip changes a line's ~100-200 pF by <= 0.4 %: lost in the noise |
| 2 | Listen for the mains hum a finger brings, every other line at 0 V | Strong and position-dependent: the finger moves the lines it sits on |
| 3 | 9 spots; digital inputs vs ADC | Digital inputs never see it (the hum stays below the input threshold); the ADC separates 7 of 9 spots |
| 4 | Short listening gaps while Pine runs | Nothing: the lines still carry charge from the scan, which swamps the finger. 8 ms gaps flicker visibly |
| 5 | **Drain first**: every line to 0 V for 5 us, then float L0-L6 (analog mode, the lightest load) 20 us-2 ms | All 9 spots stand out, each with its own line pattern, rest reading flat (noise < 8 counts); 0.2 ms of float is enough. Board on a laptop's USB port |
| Driver | Drain gaps in the scan interrupt (2 per refresh, 0.23 ms, ~5 % of LED time), pattern match + interpolation | Powerbank: **no signal at all**. Wall charger: total rise 10-60 counts in one run, 7-14 in the next, against ~1 at rest; 3-4 of 9 spots placed right |

**Why it depends on the supply.** The lines don't sense the finger's capacitance (run 1); they sense the 50 Hz hum
a person carries, measured against the board's ground. On a laptop the board's ground is tied to mains and the
difference is hundreds of counts. A USB wall charger links its output to mains only through a small capacitor, so
the difference is weaker and changes with the charger and with how the person sits. On a powerbank the board floats
along with the person and there is no difference to measure.

**Why not keep it as an extra.** At 7-14 counts a touch is only 3-7x the rest noise: a threshold low enough to
catch it is close to false touches (a wrong rest level made the tree "touched" with no hand on it in the last run),
and weak patterns are too flat to tell the spots apart. Nothing on rev A raises the signal much: a 2 ms float gives
~2x and costs visible LED time. A feature that works on some chargers and not others would read as a fault.

Found along the way:
- The PY32F003 has two comparators (COMP1 on PA1 = L1, COMP2 on PB6 = L11, against 1/4 Vrefint ~0.3 V). They do
  see the hum at a ~0.3 V threshold (experiment 5), but add nothing the ADC doesn't give.
- With the probe attached, SWDIO (line L8) is held high: any build that pulls lines low lights the L8 LEDs through
  the probe. The experiments ran with SWDIO / SWCLK unplugged and read their results once at the end.
- Polling the probe while the board runs pulsed RESET through the probe's wire and restarted the chip.

**Rev B:** a whole-tree touch would need dedicated electrodes (a copper pad or ring per zone) on free ADC pins, which
the 20-pin PY32F003 doesn't have, or a self-capacitance touch IC. The touch star stays the control: it measures
capacitance, so it works on any supply. The experiment code is in git history (commits `21a415c`, `b18460c`,
`d70cac7`, `6f3f453`, `cee5f76`), and was removed from the tree afterwards.

## Pine's bond

Saved to the last 1 KB of flash, an 8-page ring (128-byte pages; each save erases the next page and writes
{magic, sequence, bond}). The simulator saves every 5 s; the tree saves when the bond moved by 0.02 or more, at most
every 15 minutes, to spare the flash. Checked on the board: three saves rotated through pages 2-4 with rising
sequence numbers, and after a reset Pine came back with the stored bond (0.392 instead of the default 0.3). The LEDs
go dark for the ~4 ms page erase.
